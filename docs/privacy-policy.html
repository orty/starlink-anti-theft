Privacy Policy — Starlink Guard

Last updated: 18 August 2026

Starlink Guard is an anti-theft alarm for a Starlink satellite dish. It runs entirely on your phone and talks only to your own dish over your own local network.

The short version

The app collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no crash reporting. Nothing is sent to the developer, ever. The developer has no server and receives no data from this app.

What the app reads

While monitoring, the app reads the following from the Starlink dish on your local network at 192.168.100.1:

  • Orientation — boresight azimuth, elevation and mast tilt angle
  • Alignment and motor state, and the dish's own movement alerts
  • The dish hardware identifier (serial number)
  • GPS coordinates reported by the dish, when your dish and service plan expose them

This information is stored on your device only: a baseline of the dish's resting position, and a log of alarm events. Both stay in the app's private storage and are deleted when you uninstall the app. None of it leaves your phone unless you explicitly turn on the webhook feature below.

The optional webhook

The app can send an alert to a web address of your choosing when the alarm fires. This is off by default. It does nothing until you enter a URL yourself.

If you enable it, the app sends a small JSON message to the address you entered, containing the event type, which measurement changed, its previous and current values, the dish identifier, and a timestamp. That destination is chosen and controlled entirely by you — it may be your own server, or a third-party notification service. The developer is not a party to that transfer and never receives a copy. Whatever service you point it at will handle that data under its own privacy policy, so please review it. To stop all outbound transmission, clear the webhook URL.

Location

The app does not request access to your phone's location and cannot read it. Any coordinates involved come from the Starlink dish itself and are used only to detect that the dish has been moved from where you armed it.

Permissions and why they exist

  • Network and Wi-Fi state — to find the dish on the local network and keep the connection bound to Wi-Fi rather than mobile data.
  • Foreground service (connected device) — to keep monitoring the dish while the app is not on screen. This is why a persistent notification is shown.
  • Notifications and full-screen intent — to raise the alarm screen when the dish moves, including over the lock screen.
  • Do Not Disturb access — so the alarm can be heard when the phone is silenced. An alarm you cannot hear is not an alarm.
  • Wake lock and vibrate — to sound and vibrate the alarm reliably.
  • Start on boot — to resume monitoring after the phone restarts, if it was armed.

Children

The app is a utility for satellite-internet hardware and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
